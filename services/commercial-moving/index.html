How we do it

The parts of this service that actually decide whether it goes well.

The move happens when the office is empty

Evenings, weekends, holiday weekends. Downtime is the real cost of an office move, not the invoice, and every hour your team cannot work is worth more than the difference between two movers and three.

Labeled by desk and floor, not by room

Each workstation gets a number. Monitors, docks, chairs and pedestals carry that number. On the other end the crew places by number, so nobody spends Monday morning hunting for their second screen.

Building paperwork before the truck

Certificates of insurance naming the building, loading dock reservations, elevator bookings, after-hours access. Both buildings, both ends. This is the part that delays office moves, and we do it at no charge.

IT gear packed to power on, not just to survive

Cables coiled and bagged to the machine they belong to, monitors in padded crates, servers handled last out and first in. Tell us who your IT contact is and we coordinate the shutdown and restart windows with them.

Questions we get every week

How much downtime should we plan for?

For most offices under fifty people, a Friday evening load and Saturday delivery means the team is working Monday. Larger moves are phased across two weekends so half the company is always live.

Can you provide a certificate of insurance for our building?

Yes, at no charge and usually the same day you ask. We carry general liability, workers' compensation and up to $1 million in cargo coverage, which meets the requirements we have seen across office buildings in all three of our markets.

Do you handle server racks and network equipment?

We move them. We do not unrack, decommission or recable your network — that stays with your IT team or vendor, and we schedule around them.

What about the furniture we are not taking?

We can haul it away or deliver it to a donation site. It is quoted separately because disposal fees vary by city and by what it is.

How far ahead should we book?

Four to six weeks for anything over twenty people, mostly because building elevator calendars are the constraint, not ours.
